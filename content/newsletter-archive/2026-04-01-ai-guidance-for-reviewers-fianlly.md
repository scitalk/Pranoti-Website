You've probably seen of "AI guidance for scientists" but have you heard of the "AI guidance for grant proposal *evaluators*"?!

The European Research Council, the largest European funding agency with € 17 billion budget, has FINALLY released "**The use of AI in grant proposal evaluation**" guidelines for ERC panel members and remote reviewers.

**TL;DR -****Your proposals will be read by humans.** AI use in the review process is extremely limited — essentially, reviewers can use it to improve the clarity of their written feedback. That's it.

#### Here's the summary of the do's & don'ts for you:

✅ **Language and grammar polishing**, provided no proposal content is shared with the AI tool, and the reviewer's own arguments and judgement drive the content entirely.  
✅ Searching for **general information on a proposal's topic** using AI tools (as long as the proposal itself isn't uploaded).  
❌ Using AI to **summarise a proposal**, even on a local server. Understanding the proposal in full is a core reviewer task — it cannot be delegated.  
❌ Using AI to **assess scientific merit or novelty**, regardless of data privacy settings. Forming scientific judgement is a human responsibility.  
❌ Generating a **draft review**, even if the reviewer edits it afterwards. Drafting involves evaluative judgement — that's the line.  
❌ Asking AI to check whether a review is **"missing important points."** Determining what matters in a review is itself part of the evaluator's job.  
❌ **Uploading any part of a proposal to an online tool** — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — regardless of intent. This breaches confidentiality and data protection rules.

#### Why this matters for you

**Your proposal will be read by a human.** A human who has now been formally reminded that they are responsible for their own assessment — fully, independently, and from the page alone.

**That raises the bar on clarity.** A panel member working without AI assistance needs to grasp your research vision quickly and independently. The science has to land on its own terms. Clear, precise scientific communication is not a nice-to-have. It is what makes your proposal reviewable.

These **guidelines will evolve**. But having formal, published guidance for evaluators — not just applicants — is a meaningful step forward for transparency in the funding process.

Speaking of which — do you actually know **which AI tools you're allowed to use at work, and for what?** It's worth checking with your IT department or a colleague who's looked into this. And if you can't find the answers then it's about time to raise a red flag to your executive team.

What this means for scientists now.

Hello hellooo,

*"LinkedIn feels icky — what's the next platform scientists should be on?"*

An professor based in Netherlands asked me this question in one of our recent calls.

It's a fair question. I've watched scientists migrate from Twitter to LinkedIn, and now LinkedIn is filling up with AI-generated noise, algorithm unpredictability, and the pressure to post constantly just to stay visible.

But here's what I actually think is happening — and why it changes everything about how you approach your online presence.

**We are moving from platform-driven connections to people-driven ecosystems.**

Right now, the platform decides who finds you. You go where the crowd is — LinkedIn, because everyone is on LinkedIn. The platform is the middleman between you and your audience.

That is starting to shift.

A special welcome to new joiners of this newsletter. 🤗  
My name is Pranoti and I'm the founder of [The Science Talk](https://thesciencetalk.com/).  
My mission is to empower and enable scientists to share their stories in digital spaces with confidence and ease. 🧡

The trend I'm seeing — and it's not unique to science, it's happening everywhere — is that people are increasingly choosing to follow *people*, not platforms. They find someone interesting, and they enter that person's universe directly: their newsletter, their website, their podcast, their community. Substack is the clearest current example of this, but it won't be the last. New individual-driven spaces will keep emerging.

What this means for you: **the platform you're on matters less than the foundation you build.**

If your entire visibility strategy depends on LinkedIn's algorithm, you're one platform shift away from starting over. But if you've built a platform-agnostic personal brand — one rooted in your voice, your audience, and your expertise — you move with the trends instead of being swept away by them.

Because sustainable visibility isn't about being everywhere. It's about showing up in the right place, in a way that feels like *you* — not like a task on your to-do list.

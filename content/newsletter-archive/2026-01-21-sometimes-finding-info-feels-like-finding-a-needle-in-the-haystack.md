Helloo!

*Sometimes finding useful information in dense pdfs feels like looking for a needle in the haystack.*

This happens with me quite often because I've to **keep track of research projects across Europe** - European Research Council (ERC), European Innovation Council (EIC), UKRI, NNF (Denmark), FNR (Luxembourg) to name a few. **Complete list** [here](https://thesciencetalk.com/science-communication-work-packages-research-grants/).

**ERC** alone has 6 open calls each year - Starting, Consolidator, Proof of concept (twice a year), Advanced and Synergy - and the **information** needed to prepare the proposal is **scattered** across three work programme (2025, 2026, 2027) pdfs, ERC YouTube channel, ERC info webinars, ERC webpages and the list goes on.

And so when I want to check evaluation criteria for ERC Synergy 2027 grant, the info could be in the work programmes 2025 (78 page pdf), 2026 work programme (72 page pdf) or in a blogpost about changes to the 2026 & 2027 work programme.

I know where this is going cause this happened to you as well... frantically clicking around with 10 open tabs, multiple pdfs. No amount of coffee can help with this and **searching with "control+F" will only get you so far**.  

And then NotebookLM entered my TechStack. 🤩

A special welcome to new joiners of this newsletter. 🤗  
My name is Pranoti and I am the founder of [The Science Talk](https://thesciencetalk.com/).  
My mission is to empower and enable scientists to share their own stories in digital spaces with confidence and ease. 🧡

See, I'm not suggesting that NotebookLM is THE answer to this operational chaos. But it definitely is a reliable tool to extract & consolidate information from all types of digital formats - video, audio, txt, websites, pdfs.

I've been testing **NotebookLM features** for the past 15 months and **documented my experience** along the way as part of[*AI Labs - The Science Talk Experiment**s*](https://thesciencetalk.com/ai-labs-the-science-talk-experiments/) series. This tool from Google is quite impressive and does more than just extract, organise and consolidate information from 50+ sources. Almost every time NotebookLM find me a needle in the haystack.  

So give it a shot, it's definitely worth your time.

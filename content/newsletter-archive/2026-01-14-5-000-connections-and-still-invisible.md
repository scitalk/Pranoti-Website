A special welcome to new joiners of this newsletter. 🤗  
My name is Pranoti and I am the founder of [The Science Talk](https://thesciencetalk.com/).  
My mission is to empower and enable scientists to share their stories in digital spaces with confidence and ease. 🧡

Here's the thing. I've been training scientists  since 2019, and I keep seeing the same pattern. Brilliant researchers doing everything the social media gurus tell them — posting consistently, engaging with comments, growing their network — and wondering why nothing's moving.

The problem isn't their content. It's that they skipped straight to step four.

Most trainings teach you HOW to post. Hooks, hashtags, consistency, all that jazz. Super useful! But that's implementation. That's decorating the house before you've poured the foundation.

What actually matters first:

**Your career goal** — and I don't mean "increase my visibility" (that's not a goal, that's a vibe). I mean: Are you positioning for a faculty role? Trying to attract collaborators? Thinking about industry?

**Your audience** — the actual humans who can help you get there. Not "other scientists" but specific decision-makers and opinion leaders in your field.

**Your platform** — and here's where it gets interesting. LinkedIn isn't automatically the answer for everyone. A postdoc job-hunting and a tenure-track PI building thought leadership? Completely different strategies.

![](https://storage.mlcdn.com/account_image/27434/uZS4jt4cVFZxniVDDWhyEXY3eVbzSNCFyhoAPoXL.png)

Get this sequence wrong and you end up posting into the void. Great content, wrong eyeballs. (I've done this. It's frustrating and confusing and makes you want to give up entirely.)

One of my clients got 80,000+ views on a single post on LinkedIn The post was about their research paper. Not clever hacks — just clarity on who needed to see it.

## Crystals, Comics, Calculations, Chemistry ft. Amber Lim

## In Other Words

Her last published paper on the two temperature-dependent polymorphs of IrIn3: the cheap DFT chemical-pressure method hinted that atoms vibrate into void spaces in the high-temperature structure, and phonon calculations then predicted the phase-transition temperature at ~400 °C vs the experimental 350 °C.

## Key Takeaways

- Computational chemist (PhD, University of Wisconsin–Madison) studying why complex intermetallic crystals, made purely of metals, with covalent, ionic and metallic bonding at once, adopt the structures they do.
- Wanted to be a high-school chemistry teacher; a Welch high-school research program and then arc-melting/flux synthesis (samples occasionally exploding) led her to computation.
- Predicting something close to experiment from basic, inexpensive calculations 'was really amazing'.
- Favourite non-research part: scientists are diverse and creative, a department zine, D&D, pottery, and her own anime and science-communication comics.
- Three wishes: had learned computer science / documentation / data hygiene earlier, sought mentors outside her group sooner, and research funding that keeps up with inflation.

## In Other Words

Her current work growing self-healing Aurivillius ferroelectric thin films: using in-situ second-harmonic generation to watch polarization during growth (revealing a two-step behaviour), then engineering bismuth ferrite into the fatigue-free framework to get a net, non-degrading polarization.

## In this episode

- What ferroelectric materials are, explained by comparison with fridge magnets
- Why the same ceramic can respond to pressure, heat and light, and what that means for sensors
- How a summer internship at CERN on bismuth ferrite took Ipek from Sabancı University in Istanbul to a PhD at ETH Zurich
- Why ferroelectrics wear out after millions of uses, and how "self-healing" Aurivillius crystals avoid this fatigue
- How shining a laser on a film while it grows let her team watch its polarisation change for the first time
- Her three wishes: more talk between scientific fields, more science communication with the public, and time for side projects driven by curiosity

## Key Takeaways

- PhD student at ETH Zurich growing ferroelectric oxide thin films (1–15 nm), materials that combine piezo-, pyro-, photovoltaic and ferroelastic behaviour in one.
- Ferroelectric memories 'fatigue' after ~a million–billion cycles as defects pin the polarization; Aurivillius crystals self-heal this via charged intermediate layers.
- A CERN internship on multiferroic bismuth ferrite (switching magnetism with small electric fields, saving energy) set her direction.
- Likes that materials science makes her mindful of everything around her, and that science teaches how to find reliable information.
- Three wishes: more interdisciplinary conversation, science communication as a default institutional expectation, and free time for 'crazy' side projects.

## About the guest

Ipek Efe is a PhD Student at ETH Zurich in Switzerland, where she develops ferroelectric thin films with novel properties. Her doctoral project focuses on Aurivillius ferroelectrics, a family of crystals that self-heal electrostatically, and on engineering their growth to achieve a net polarisation. She holds a BSc in Materials Science and Nanoengineering from Sabanci University, Istanbul, and an MSc in Materials Science from ETH Zurich.

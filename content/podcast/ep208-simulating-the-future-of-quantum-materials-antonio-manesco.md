## In Other Words

A just-posted arXiv paper proposing the basic building block of a fermionic quantum computer, a device built from superconductors and semiconductors that behaves like interacting electrons, so simulating many-electron systems avoids the qubit–electron mismatch.

{{< split image="/digital-content-hub/images/Antonio%20208.jpg" side="right" bg="#fffafc" >}}
## Key Takeaways

- Theoretical/computational physicist (postdoc at TU Delft) simulating mesoscopic and nanoscale electronic devices in Python on university clusters.
- Started as an experimentalist (cutting superconducting ceramics), disliked it, moved to theory, but still needs an experimental motivation to start a project.
- Gave a clear explanation of topological insulators: the gap must close and reopen at the interface with vacuum, creating a robust metallic edge good for low-dissipation transport.
- Quantum computers won't be personal devices soon; the payoff is solving currently-intractable problems, accelerating science.
- Three wishes: stable postdoc careers, an end to geographical/general bias in academia (he faced personal attacks in reviews), and a real open-science culture (no €10k fees, shared code and data).

## About the guest

Antonio Manesco is a Postdoc Researcher at Delft University of Technology in the Netherlands, working on mesoscopic and nano electronics: how electrons behave in low-dimensional devices. He holds a bachelor's in Engineering Physics and a PhD from Brazil, from the University of São Paulo.

## Space Travel, Heavy Ions, Electric Cars ft. Dimosthenis Katsis

## In Other Words

An Army Research Lab project using radioactive tritium to make copper-doped zinc-sulphide tubes glow green, coupled to cylindrical solar cells, creating tiny undetectable 20-year 'tritium batteries' for long-life field sensors.

## Key Takeaways

- Director of Power Electronics at PowerAmerica, an ~89-member US/Europe/Asia consortium sharing wide-bandgap semiconductor (SiC, GaN) technology for electrification of transport, aerospace and medicine.
- Material science is what makes power electronics commercially viable, encapsulants that prevent arcing, corona and dendrite bridges in 800 V EV battery packs matter as much as the chips.
- Compact/mobile accelerator and wide-bandgap tech could move capabilities (cancer therapy, sensing) to places that can't currently afford or import them.
- Career: Virginia Tech solar-car team, GE, PhD, Blue Origin (space-radiation testing; lunar-rock solar cells), consulting on hybrid-electric aircraft.
- Three wishes: fund basic research with forward/long-term thinking that also directs climate and technology money to the people least able to afford the fixes; rebuild scientist–public trust.

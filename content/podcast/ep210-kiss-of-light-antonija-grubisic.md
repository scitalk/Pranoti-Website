Antonija Grubisic-Cabo first came on Under the Microscope in 2020 as a postdoc in Sweden. She is back as a tenure-track assistant professor at the University of Groningen. She studies 2D materials and topological insulators out of equilibrium, hitting them with laser light to create phases that last only femtoseconds, and she co-developed KISS, a way to make large 2D layers using a single crystal of gold.

## About the guest

Antonija Grubisic-Cabo is an assistant professor at the [Zernike Institute for Advanced Materials, University of Groningen](https://www.rug.nl/staff/a.grubisic-cabo/?lang=en), where she leads research on 2D and quantum materials using ARPES and time-resolved ARPES. Since this episode she has received the [2025 Minerva Prize](https://www.rug.nl/research/zernike/news/newsitems/202511-minerva-agc?lang=en) from the Dutch Physical Society and the Dutch Physics Council for her work on quantum and two-dimensional materials.

## Why it matters

Antonija explains that using fast light to switch topological materials could lead to very fast devices, and ones that don't overheat, which also means better battery life because less energy is lost as heat. Her KISS method tackles a practical problem: sticky tape gives the nicest quality 2D flakes, but they are very small, and surface science needs large, uniform, clean surfaces. Doing everything in vacuum also means air-sensitive materials never touch air. Groups in Denmark, at KTH and at UBC now use the method. Her case for fundamental research: nobody knows what these materials will be used for in a few years. Graphene is not a very good transistor, but it turned out to be an excellent heat sink and useful in touch screens.

## Terms explained

In the guest's own words, with a link to the moment they explain it.

- **Spin and valleytronics:** in hexagonal materials like tungsten disulfide, neighbouring corners of the hexagon have opposite spin, so circularly polarised light can select electrons of one spin or the other. *(Antonija at [06:41](https://youtu.be/cjKV4TRIDFM?t=401))*
- **ARPES:** angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy. Shine light, usually from a synchrotron, to knock electrons out, capture them, and get pictures of a material's electronic bands. *(Antonija at [10:59](https://youtu.be/cjKV4TRIDFM?t=659))*
- **KISS exfoliation:** instead of sticky tape, a clean single crystal of gold is pressed against a layered material inside ultra-high vacuum, and a large 2D layer peels off onto the gold. *(Antonija at [18:28](https://youtu.be/cjKV4TRIDFM?t=1108))*

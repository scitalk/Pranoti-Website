## Nano hockey sticks to win over diseases ft. Steve Streets

## In Other Words

Making tiny non-spherical block-copolymer 'sticks' (nanofibers via crystallisation-driven self-assembly) functionalised to bind DNA on their surface, the first non-spherical 'micelleplex', as gene-delivery vehicles, tested by making brain-cancer cells glow with firefly luciferase.

## Key Takeaways

- Postdoc at University of Victoria making precision non-spherical nanomaterials (10 nm–10 µm), because at the nanoscale everything wants to be a sphere.
- Career built on taking opportunities: a 'most improved chemistry student' award led to an industry job, then a Bristol CDT PhD where students pick their own project.
- Shifted from small-molecule anti-cancer sugars to polymer drug delivery because getting medicine to the right place (e.g. the brain) is the real bottleneck.
- Values the mental workout, freedom to choose directions, and better work-life balance in his group.
- Three wishes: a streamlined funding system, better public understanding of chemistry, and Silicon-Valley-style quality of life in academia.

## In Other Words

Discovering a new hexagonal form of diamond ('Lonsdaleite', predicted ~58% harder than normal diamond) made at room temperature just by squeezing, a career-changing moment.

{{< split image="/digital-content-hub/images/Jodie.jpg" side="right" bg="#fffafc" >}}
## Key Takeaways

- Condensed-matter physicist at ANU who 'gets matter and squishes it', using diamond anvil cells up to ~100 GPa to force silicon, germanium and carbon into new crystal structures.
- Silicon has 13 possible structures; new phases could make solar cells absorb more of the spectrum, but industrial scale-up of high-pressure synthesis is the key challenge.
- Brought microscopy/spectroscopy (esp. TEM on recovered 'salami-slice' samples) into a high-pressure field that traditionally stopped at the in-situ X-ray measurement.
- Loves lab-based teaching and breaking the stereotype that physics ability is innate or gendered.
- Three wishes: more women/diversity in physics, a far more efficient funding/grant system, and a fix for impact-factor-driven publishing.

## About the guest

Jodie Bradby is a Professor at The Australian National University, where she studies how materials change when squeezed to high pressure in a diamond anvil cell. She found how to make lonsdaleite, a new type of diamond, at room temperature. She studied Applied Physics at RMIT, did her PhD at ANU, became a full professor in 2018, and was President of the Australian Institute of Physics in 2020–21.

## In Other Words

Figuring out during his postdoc how to make quantum dots without strain, overturning the assumption that stress is the driving force for dot formation; he grew the sample overnight and saw the proof of a new kind of 'golden dot' at 6 a.m., and 20 years later strain-free dots are taking over the field.

## Key Takeaways

- Professor at Johannes Kepler University Linz making epitaxial quantum dots (~50 nm wide, ~7 nm tall), 'chocolate chips in a 5-km muffin', for optoelectronic and quantum-light applications.
- Colloidal quantum dots (spherical, 2023 Nobel) go into bright TV screens; epitaxial dots are better for lasers and single/entangled-photon sources like the QuanTour source.
- The QuanTour 'bullseye' cavity is a specialised high-efficiency lens with a back mirror that funnels ~90% of emitted single photons into a fibre.
- During his PhD he peeled graphite with Scotch tape to calibrate an STM and threw the flakes away, 'I missed the Nobel Prize'.
- One wish: far less bureaucracy in grants and reporting. Co-leads QuanTour and the Quantum Science Austria (QuantA) fundamental-science cluster.

We signed the European Commission's **Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content** — under **Section 2, as a deployer of generative AI systems**. That puts The Science Talk in the same signatory pool as Bulgari, Getty Images, Lenovo, and Lufthansa, on a framework whose Section 1 side counts Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Mistral among its signatories.

We are doing this two months before we have to.

## Why this, why now

The AI Act's transparency obligations — the rules requiring AI-generated and AI-modified content to be marked and disclosed — become legally binding on **2 August 2026**. The Code of Practice is the Commission's sanctioned route to compliance: drafted by independent experts, assessed as adequate by the Commission and the AI Board, and built to give signatories "a streamlined, predictable and legally certain pathway" regardless of which national authority ends up supervising them.

By the Commission's own count, roughly **190 organizations** had signed as of end of July 2026. We're one of them, and about half of that signatory base is small, recently founded companies — this isn't a club reserved for platforms with in-house legal teams. It's a framework built to scale down as well as up.

## What Section 1 and Section 2 actually mean

The Code splits obligations by role, not by size:

**Section 1 — Providers.** This covers providers of generative AI systems, the companies building marking and detection tooling, and AI model providers themselves. It's the technical backbone: the commitments that make labeling and detection actually possible at the infrastructure level. Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Mistral sit here — the organizations whose models and systems generate the content in the first place.

**Section 2 — Deployers.** This covers organizations that put generative AI systems to work in production — using AI tools to create, adapt, or publish content for real audiences. Deployers commit to marking AI-generated content appropriately, disclosing its use where it matters, and building practices that support public trust rather than eroding it. This is where The Science Talk signed.

The distinction matters for how we frame this: we're not a lab building AI models. We're an agency that uses AI systems in science communication work, and Section 2 is the commitment that we do that transparently — every time, not just when convenient.

## What it says about how we operate

Science communication runs on trust. The moment an audience suspects an AI-generated graphic, script, or summary was passed off as fully human-made without disclosure, that trust is gone — and it doesn't come back with an apology. Signing this Code, ahead of the legal deadline, is a public commitment that The Science Talk treats that trust as non-negotiable, not as a compliance checkbox to tick once the law forces it.

It's also a signal about pace. The legal obligation lands 2 August 2026. The Commission's implementation task forces don't even launch until September 2026. We signed in the window before either of those dates — while most organizations in our space are still deciding whether this applies to them.

## What's next

Task forces to support Code implementation launch in September 2026. We'll be tracking that process and will update our own disclosure practices as the framework matures — the same way we've tracked the AI Act's high-risk timeline shifts and Article 4 literacy obligations elsewhere on this site.

For now: if you work with The Science Talk, you can expect AI-generated content to be marked as such, consistently and without you having to ask.

Most SMEs and agencies I talk to have mentally filed the **EU AI Act** as someone else's problem — 180+ articles of dense legal text, clearly written for hyperscalers and regulators, not a five-person creative studio or a research consultancy. That's the wrong read. This is a practical **EU AI Act checklist for small business**: six articles decide almost everything a small team needs to know, and this is the one-page map to them.

I've written a full deep-dive on each of these six separately. This piece is the index — read it first, then follow whichever link matches where you actually are.

> **Key takeaways:** Two of these six obligations (Articles 4 and 5) have already applied since February 2025 — not a future deadline. One article (62) exists purely to help you, not burden you. And if your agency rebrands, fine-tunes, or repurposes any AI tool for a client, Article 25 is the one clause most teams have never read but most need to.

## The EU AI Act checklist for small business: 6 articles at a glance

| Article | What it demands | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 3** | Are you a provider or a deployer? Defines the roles the rest of the Act hangs on | Everyone — the gateway question |
| **Art. 4** | Ensure "sufficient AI literacy" among staff, freelancers, contractors | Anyone with a team, live since Feb 2025 |
| **Art. 5** | Red lines banned outright — workplace emotion recognition, dark-pattern design, face-scraping | Everyone, live since Feb 2025 |
| **Art. 25** | You can become the legal "provider" by rebranding, modifying, or repurposing a tool | Agencies doing white-label or custom AI work |
| **Art. 50** | Disclose AI-generated content clearly, before the reader reaches it | Anyone publishing AI-assisted content, from 2 Aug 2026 |
| **Art. 62** | Free support: sandbox priority, reduced fees, AI Office templates | SMEs and start-ups — the part most teams never claim |

## Art. 3 — Are you even in scope?

Before anything else, [Article 3](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-3) sets out the roles the entire Act is built on: a **provider** develops an AI system and places it on the market; a **deployer** uses one under its own authority. Most of the Act's obligations hinge entirely on which side of that line you sit on.

For a deeper walkthrough of how to classify your own tools, see [the official 7-element test for what counts as an "AI system"](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-ai-system-definition-7-element-test/) and [the provider-vs-deployer test for the GPAI Code of Practice](/ai-guides/gpai-code-of-practice-provider-vs-deployer/).

## Art. 4 — You already have a literacy duty

[Article 4](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-4) requires providers and deployers to ensure "a sufficient level of AI literacy" among staff and anyone operating AI systems on their behalf — including freelancers and contractors. This has applied since **2 February 2025**, six months earlier than most teams assume.

Full breakdown, including what "sufficient" actually means in practice: [Article 4 for creator agencies and small teams](/perspectives/eu-ai-act-article-4-creator-agencies-small-teams/).

## Art. 5 — The red lines that apply to everyone, today

[Article 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5) has also applied since 2 February 2025, and bans specific practices outright — no risk assessment, no exception for small operators. The four that realistically touch an ordinary tool stack: workplace emotion recognition, manipulative dark-pattern design, untargeted facial-image scraping, and biometric categorisation by protected traits. Two more bans — on AI-generated non-consensual intimate images and CSAM — were added by the July 2026 Omnibus amendment and apply from 2 December 2026.

Full checklist to run your own tools through: [the 4 Article 5 practices you're banned from using](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-prohibited-practices-article-5/).

## Art. 25 — How your agency could accidentally become a "provider"

[Article 25](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-25) is the one clause most agencies never read. Rebrand a tool under your own name, substantially modify it, or repurpose a general-purpose model into a high-risk use, and you can legally become the "provider" — inheriting the full conformity-assessment and documentation burden that was supposed to sit with your vendor.

Full breakdown of the three triggers and the contract clause that decides who carries the risk: [Article 25 — when you become the AI "provider"](/perspectives/eu-ai-act-article-25-agency-becomes-provider/).

## Art. 50 — The one deadline everyone's heard of

[Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) requires clear, distinguishable disclosure when content is AI-generated or manipulated, before a reader's first exposure to it — not buried in end credits or a linked policy page. This is the transparency obligation most creator-facing coverage of the Act focuses on, and it applies from **2 August 2026**.

Full placement guidance for blog posts, social media, video, and podcasts: [how to add AI disclosures that comply with the EU AI Act](/ai-guides/ai-generated-content-disclosure-eu-ai-act/).

## Art. 62 — The support most teams don't know they can claim

[Article 62](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-62) is the part of the Act that runs in the small team's favour: priority access to national regulatory sandboxes, reduced conformity-assessment fees, a direct advice channel with your national authority, and free standardised templates from the AI Office.

Full breakdown of how to actually claim each measure: [Article 62 — free support SMEs can claim](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-sme-support-article-62/).

## Common questions on the EU AI Act for small business

**Does the EU AI Act actually apply to a business with under 10 people?**
Yes. Size doesn't exempt you from the Act's obligations — it changes how those obligations are enforced. [Article 99(6)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-99) caps fines for SMEs and start-ups at the *lower* of the applicable percentage or amount thresholds, rather than the higher figures large companies face, and Article 62 gives you support most teams never claim.

**What's the single most-missed obligation for small teams?**
Article 25. Most agencies assume they're a "deployer" using someone else's tool, and never realise that rebranding, substantially modifying, or repurposing a tool can legally make them the "provider" instead — with the full compliance burden that entails.

**Do I need to worry about fines right now?**
For most small teams, no — the highest-profile deadlines (Article 50 transparency, most Annex III high-risk obligations) either haven't arrived yet or have been pushed to December 2027 under the 2026 Omnibus amendments. Articles 4 and 5 are the exception: both have applied since February 2025.

**Where do I start if I've done nothing about this yet?**
Run the [7-element test](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-ai-system-definition-7-element-test/) on your actual tool stack first — it tells you whether the Act applies to a given tool at all, before you worry about any specific article.

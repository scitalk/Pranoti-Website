Most SMEs and agencies file the **EU AI Act** as someone else's problem. It has more than 180 articles of dense legal text. The text looks written for hyperscalers and regulators, not a five-person creative studio or a research consultancy. That view is wrong. This is a practical **EU AI Act checklist for small business**. Six articles decide almost everything a small team needs to know. This is the one-page map to them.

We have written a full deep-dive on each of these six articles separately. This piece is the index. Read it first, then follow whichever link matches where you are.

> **Key takeaways:** Two of these six obligations (Articles 4 and 5) apply since February 2025. That date is not a future deadline, though the July 2026 Omnibus has since lightened what Article 4 asks of you. One article (62) exists only to help you, not to burden you. If your agency rebrands, fine-tunes, or repurposes any AI tool for a client, read Article 25. Most teams have never read it, but most need it.

## The EU AI Act checklist for small business: 6 articles at a glance

| Article | What it demands | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 3** | Are you a provider or a deployer? This article defines the roles the rest of the Act depends on | Everyone: the gateway question |
| **Art. 4** | AI literacy among staff, freelancers, and contractors — simplified by the July 2026 Omnibus | Anyone with a team, in force since Feb 2025 |
| **Art. 5** | This article bans red lines outright: workplace emotion recognition, dark-pattern design, face-scraping | Everyone, in force since Feb 2025 |
| **Art. 25** | You can become the legal "provider" by rebranding, modifying, or repurposing a tool | Agencies doing white-label or custom AI work |
| **Art. 50** | Disclose AI-generated content clearly, before the reader reaches it | Anyone publishing AI-assisted content, from 2 Aug 2026 |
| **Art. 62** | Free support: sandbox priority, reduced fees, AI Office templates | SMEs and start-ups: the part most teams never claim |

## Art. 3 — Are you even in scope?

First, check your role. [Article 3](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-3) sets out the roles the entire Act is built on. A **provider** develops an AI system and places it on the market. A **deployer** uses one under its own authority. Most of the Act's obligations depend on which side of that line you sit on.

For a deeper walkthrough of how to classify your own tools, see [the official 7-element test for what counts as an "AI system"](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-ai-system-definition-7-element-test/) and [the provider-vs-deployer test for the GPAI Code of Practice](/ai-guides/gpai-code-of-practice-provider-vs-deployer/).

## Art. 4 — You already have a literacy duty

[Article 4](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-4) covers AI literacy among staff and anyone who operates AI systems on your behalf, including freelancers and contractors. It has applied since **2 February 2025**, six months earlier than most teams assume — but the July 2026 Digital Omnibus, [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng), simplified the company-level requirement, shifting the leading role onto the Commission and Member States. Most coverage still describes the stricter original wording.

Full breakdown, including what "sufficient" actually means in practice: [Article 4 for creator agencies and small teams](/perspectives/eu-ai-act-article-4-creator-agencies-small-teams/).

## Art. 5 — The red lines that apply to everyone, today

[Article 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5) has also applied since 2 February 2025. It bans specific practices outright, with no risk assessment and no exception for small operators. Four bans realistically touch an ordinary tool stack: workplace emotion recognition, manipulative dark-pattern design, untargeted facial-image scraping, and biometric categorization by protected traits. The July 2026 Omnibus amendment added two more bans, on AI-generated non-consensual intimate images and CSAM. Those two bans apply from 2 December 2026.

Full checklist to run your own tools through: [the 4 Article 5 practices you're banned from using](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-prohibited-practices-article-5/).

## Art. 25 — How your agency could accidentally become a "provider"

[Article 25](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-25) is the one clause most agencies never read. If you rebrand a tool under your own name, substantially modify it, or repurpose a general-purpose model into a high-risk use, you can legally become the "provider." That status brings the full conformity-assessment and documentation burden that was meant to sit with your vendor.

Full breakdown of the three triggers and the contract clause that decides who carries the risk: [Article 25 — when you become the AI "provider"](/perspectives/eu-ai-act-article-25-agency-becomes-provider/).

## Art. 50 — The one deadline everyone's heard of

[Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) requires clear, distinguishable disclosure when content is AI-generated or manipulated. Show the disclosure before a reader's first exposure to the content, not buried in end credits or a linked policy page. This is the transparency obligation most creator-facing coverage of the Act focuses on. It applies from **2 August 2026**.

Full placement guidance for blog posts, social media, video, and podcasts: [how to add AI disclosures that comply with the EU AI Act](/ai-guides/ai-generated-content-disclosure-eu-ai-act/).

## Art. 62 — The support most teams don't know they can claim

[Article 62](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-62) is the part of the Act that works in the small team's favor. It gives priority access to national regulatory sandboxes, reduced conformity-assessment fees, a direct advice channel with your national authority, and free standardized templates from the AI Office.

Full breakdown of how to actually claim each measure: [Article 62 — free support SMEs can claim](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-sme-support-article-62/).

## Common questions on the EU AI Act for small business

**Does the EU AI Act apply to a business with under 10 people?**
Yes. Size does not exempt you from the Act's obligations. Size changes how those obligations are enforced. [Article 99(6)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-99) caps fines for SMEs and start-ups at the *lower* of the applicable percentage or amount thresholds, instead of the higher figures large companies face. Article 62 also gives you support most teams never claim.

**What is the single most-missed obligation for small teams?**
Article 25. Most agencies assume they are a "deployer" using someone else's tool. They never realize that rebranding, substantially modifying, or repurposing a tool can legally make them the "provider" instead, with the full compliance burden that status brings.

**Do I need to worry about fines right now?**
For most small teams, no. The highest-profile deadlines, such as Article 50 transparency and most Annex III high-risk obligations, either have not arrived yet or moved to December 2027 under the 2026 Omnibus amendments. Articles 4 and 5 are the exception. Both have applied since February 2025.

**Where do I start if I have done nothing about this yet?**
Run the [7-element test](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-ai-system-definition-7-element-test/) on your actual tool stack first. It tells you whether the Act applies to a given tool at all, before you check any specific article.

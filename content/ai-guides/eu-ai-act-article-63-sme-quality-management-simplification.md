Most coverage of the 2026 Digital Omnibus focused on the headline: high-risk deadlines pushed to 2027 and 2028. Underneath that, the same amendment quietly rewrote **Article 63** — and made it meaningfully more generous. What used to be a narrow carve-out for microenterprises now covers **all SMEs, including start-ups**.

If you build or substantially modify an AI system that lands in high-risk territory, this is the provision that decides how much quality-management machinery you actually have to construct. It is also one of the most misunderstood, because it looks like a general SME exemption and is not one.

## What you need before starting

- You are a **provider** of a high-risk AI system, or heading that way. If you only use third-party AI tools as sold, Article 63 does not apply to you — and neither does the obligation it relieves. The [Article 25 guide](/perspectives/eu-ai-act-article-25-agency-becomes-provider/) covers how deployers accidentally become providers.
- A view on whether any of your systems are high-risk. The [Annex III test](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-high-risk-hiring-ai-annex-iii-test/) is the quickest route.
- Your enterprise structure to hand — specifically, whether you have partner or linked enterprises. This turns out to be the condition that disqualifies most applicants.

## What changed in 2026

The original Article 63(1) opened with "Microenterprises within the meaning of Recommendation 2003/361/EC." That is a very small population — the relief was, in practice, almost theoretical.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, replaced the paragraph. It now reads:

> SMEs, including start-ups, may comply with certain elements of the quality management system required by Article 17 in a simplified manner, provided that they do not have partner enterprises or linked enterprises within the meaning of Recommendation 2003/361/EC.

Two things happened at once. The eligible population widened from microenterprises to **all SMEs including start-ups**. And the word "microenterprises" disappeared from the Commission's guidelines mandate too — the guidelines it must now produce are targeted at SME needs generally.

## What the simplification actually covers

Article 63 relieves exactly one thing: **certain elements of the quality management system under Article 17**, complied with "in a simplified manner." That is it.

The quality management system is the documented, organisation-level apparatus a high-risk provider must maintain — the policies, procedures, testing regimes, and record-keeping that demonstrate systematic compliance rather than one-off effort. For a ten-person company it is the single heaviest obligation in the Act. Being allowed to meet parts of it proportionately is genuinely material.

What Article 63 does **not** do is reduce the substantive requirements the system exists to prove.

## The condition that disqualifies most teams

The standalone requirement survived the rewrite intact: you must **not have partner enterprises or linked enterprises** within the meaning of Recommendation 2003/361/EC.

This matters more than the headcount threshold. An SME that is majority-owned by a larger group, or holds a substantial stake in another company, or sits inside a corporate structure with linked entities, does not qualify — regardless of how few people it employs. A venture-backed start-up with a significant institutional shareholder should check this carefully before assuming the relief applies.

If you fail the standalone test, Article 63 is closed to you. The other two simplifications below are not conditioned this way.

## Article 63(2): the part people skip

The second paragraph is unchanged, and it is the reason this is not a general exemption:

> Paragraph 1 of this Article shall not be interpreted as exempting those operators from fulfilling any other requirements or obligations laid down in this Regulation, including those established in Articles 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 72 and 73.

In plain terms, all of the following still apply in full:

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| 9 | Risk management system |
| 10 | Data and data governance |
| 11 | Technical documentation |
| 12 | Record-keeping and logging |
| 13 | Transparency and information for deployers |
| 14 | Human oversight |
| 15 | Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity |
| 72 | Post-market monitoring |
| 73 | Serious incident reporting |

Article 63 makes the *management system* lighter. It does not make the *system itself* less regulated.

## Two neighbouring reliefs worth claiming

Article 63 is not the only SME accommodation, and the other two are easier to qualify for because neither carries the standalone condition.

**Article 17(2) — proportionality.** The Omnibus replaced this paragraph. Implementation of the quality management system must now be "proportionate to the size of the provider's organisation, in particular, if the provider is an SME, including a start-up, or an SMC." The text adds a limit: providers must "in any event, respect the degree of rigour and the level of protection required." Proportionate, not optional.

**Article 11(1) — simplified technical documentation.** Also replaced by the Omnibus, and widened to cover small mid-caps. SMEs, start-ups and SMCs may supply the Annex IV elements in simplified form, using a form the **Commission is obliged to produce** — and which **notified bodies must accept** for conformity assessment purposes. Note this sits in Article 11, not Article 62, despite frequently being described as part of the SME support package.

Small mid-caps also now get the more favourable fine calculation under Article 99, which was previously an SME-only accommodation.

## The timing nobody mentions

Article 63 is in force today, 2 August 2026. Article 17 — the obligation it simplifies — is not.

Chapter III Section 3, which contains Article 17, was deferred by the same Omnibus amendment to **2 December 2027** for stand-alone Annex III systems and **2 August 2028** for AI embedded in already-regulated products. So the relief is legally live with nothing yet to attach to.

What *is* live today is the Commission's mandate to develop the simplification guidelines. That is the thing to watch: the guidelines will define which elements of the quality management system can be simplified, and that definition determines how much this provision is actually worth.

> Treat this as a planning input, not a compliance task. If you are designing a quality management system in 2026 for a deadline in late 2027, knowing you qualify for simplified treatment changes how much you build now.

## What you can do now

Three concrete steps, in order:

1. **Run the standalone test.** Check whether you have partner or linked enterprises under Recommendation 2003/361/EC. This is a yes/no answer, it takes ten minutes with your cap table, and it determines whether Article 63 is available to you at all.
2. **Separate the two questions.** "Do I qualify for simplified quality management?" and "am I a provider of a high-risk system?" are independent. Most small teams are deployers and never reach the second. Confirm which side you are on before investing in either.
3. **Watch for the Commission guidelines** rather than designing a full quality management system from scratch in the meantime. The guidelines are the operative detail, and they do not exist yet.

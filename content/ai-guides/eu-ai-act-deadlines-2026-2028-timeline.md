The EU AI Act's original 2 August 2026 deadline for high-risk AI obligations no longer applies. The European Parliament approved a simplification package on 16 June 2026 (423 votes in favor, 57 against, 174 abstentions) that pushed several key dates back, in some cases by well over a year. This guide gives the current, officially confirmed timeline, sourced directly from European Parliament press releases. It points to which date applies to your situation.

> **Source note:** Every date below is quoted or confirmed directly from official European Parliament press releases (europarl.europa.eu). Where a figure could not be confirmed against an official EU source, this guide leaves it out rather than including it as a guess. This page is dated 15 July 2026. Legislative dates can still shift with further procedural steps (Official Journal publication, entry into force). Check the `lastmod` date above against the official sources linked here before you rely on this for a compliance deadline.

## What you need before starting

- No technical setup. This is a reference guide to confirm which deadline applies to your organization.
- If you are unsure whether your tool or model falls under the AI Act at all, start with the [AI system definition test](/ai-guides/eu-ai-act-ai-system-definition-7-element-test/) before you check dates.

## Why the dates changed

The Commission's original 2024 timeline set 2 August 2026 as the date most high-risk AI obligations would apply. Parliament's simplification package changed that. The European Parliament's own press release states that the co-legislators intended to adopt the package "before 2 August 2026, the start date for current rules on high-risk systems." This confirms the original deadline was the trigger for the reform, not a date still in effect.

## The dates that still stand

Two dates from the original AI Act timeline are unaffected by the 2026 simplification package:

- **2 February 2025** — Prohibited practices (Article 5), AI-literacy duties, and the AI system definition (Chapters I and II) began applying.
- **2 August 2025** — Rules for general-purpose AI models, governance, notifying authorities, confidentiality, and penalties began applying.

## EU AI Act deadlines that changed in 2026

According to the European Parliament's press release on the simplification deal:

- **Stand-alone high-risk AI systems** (including biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement, and border management use cases): obligations now apply **from 2 December 2027**, not 2 August 2026.
- **High-risk AI systems used as safety components covered by EU sectoral product-safety legislation** (for example, machinery, medical devices): obligations now apply **from 2 August 2028**, not 2 August 2027.
- **Watermarking obligations on AI-generated content**: postponed to **2 December 2026**. The Commission's original proposal had set 2 February 2027, so this date is earlier than what the Commission itself proposed, though later than the original AI Act date.

> This watermarking deadline is a distinct, provider-facing obligation, separate from the Article 50(4) content-disclosure duty covered in [How to add AI disclosures that comply with the EU AI Act](/ai-guides/ai-generated-content-disclosure-eu-ai-act/). The two are easy to conflate but are not the same requirement.

## What is new in the package

Alongside the deadline changes, the package adds a new prohibition. It bans AI systems that generate non-consensual sexual or intimate content, or child sexual abuse material (CSAM), often called "nudifier" apps. Companies must bring existing systems into compliance with this ban **by 2 December 2026**.

## EU AI Act deadlines 2026–2028 at a glance

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| 2 February 2025 | Prohibited practices, AI-literacy duties, AI system definition |
| 2 August 2025 | GPAI model rules, governance, penalties |
| 16 June 2026 | Parliament approves the simplification package |
| 2 December 2026 | Watermarking obligations for AI-generated content; nudifier/CSAM ban compliance deadline |
| 2 December 2027 | Stand-alone high-risk AI system obligations |
| 2 August 2028 | High-risk AI systems embedded in EU sectoral-regulated products |

## Which date actually affects you?

- **If you provide a general-purpose AI model**, the 2 August 2025 obligations already apply to you. See [GPAI Code of Practice: Provider vs. Deployer](/ai-guides/gpai-code-of-practice-provider-vs-deployer/) to confirm whether you are a provider at all.
- **If you deploy a stand-alone high-risk AI system** (for example, in recruitment, education, or law enforcement contexts), you now have until **2 December 2027**, not 2026.
- **If your AI system is a safety component in an already-regulated product** (medical devices, machinery), your deadline is **2 August 2028**.
- **If you publish AI-generated content and need to add machine-readable watermarking**, your deadline moved to **2 December 2026**.
- **If your product could generate non-consensual intimate imagery or CSAM**, you must comply with the new prohibition by **2 December 2026**, regardless of your risk category.

## Troubleshooting

**"I thought I had until August 2026 for high-risk compliance."** That was the original deadline. The June 2026 simplification package pushed it to 2 December 2027 for stand-alone high-risk systems, or 2 August 2028 if your system is embedded in an already-regulated product.

**"Is this simplification package still just a proposal?"** No. Parliament formally approved it on 16 June 2026 with a clear majority (423-57-174). This guide does not confirm the exact Official Journal publication or entry-into-force date. Treat these as the adopted legislative dates rather than assume every procedural step is complete.

**"Does the watermarking deadline apply to me if I just write about AI, not build it?"** No. Watermarking is a provider-facing technical obligation on the AI system itself. If you publish AI-generated text or images for public-interest topics, the relevant duty is the Article 50(4) disclosure obligation covered in the linked guide above, not this one.

## What you can do now

You can now state, with a source you can point to, which AI Act deadline applies to your specific situation. Some deadlines moved later (high-risk systems) while others moved earlier than the Commission's own proposal (watermarking).

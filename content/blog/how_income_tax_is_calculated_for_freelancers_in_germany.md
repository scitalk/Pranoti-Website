If you are a full‑time freelancer in Germany, income tax is not calculated per income source. It is calculated **per person**. This distinction is small, but it explains most tax surprises freelancers experience.

> **Quick Insight:** Understanding this single principle will save you thousands in unexpected tax payments.

In particular, things become confusing when you combine freelancing income with benefits such as **Arbeitslosengeld I (AGL 1)**.

---

## The part most people miss

Germany uses a **progressive income tax system**. This means:

- You have **one personal tax rate**
- That rate depends on your **total income level**
- Not every income is taxed — but some still affect the rate

AGL 1 is the classic example.

AGL 1 is tax‑free, but it is subject to *Progressionsvorbehalt*. In plain terms: it increases your tax rate without being taxed itself.

---

## How the tax rate is determined

Your tax rate is calculated based on the following:

```
Tax rate = f(AGL 1 + freelancing profit)
```

Both amounts are added **only** to determine which tax bracket and average rate applies.

---

## What is actually taxed

The tax rate is **not** applied to that total amount.

It is applied only to your freelancing income, after deductions:

```
Taxable amount = freelancing profit − health insurance (AOK)
```

AGL 1 is never taxed.

---

## The final calculation

```
Income tax = tax rate × (freelancing profit − AOK)
```

This separation — *rate determination* versus *tax base* — is the key to understanding German income tax.

---

## Example with real numbers

| Item | Amount (€) |
|---|---:|
| Arbeitslosengeld I | 16,200 |
| Freelancing profit | 12,000 |
| Health insurance (AOK) | 3,200 |

**Tax rate is calculated on:**

```
16,200 + 12,000 = 28,200
```

**Tax is applied to:**

```
12,000 − 3,200 = 8,800
```

Result:
- AGL 1 increases the tax rate
- Health insurance reduces the taxable base
- Only freelancing profit is taxed

---

## Why this matters

Once you understand this structure:

- Business expenses become strategic, not administrative
- Health insurance contributions stop feeling like “lost money”
- Tax planning becomes predictable instead of stressful

This is not about optimising aggressively. It is about understanding the rules you are already playing by.

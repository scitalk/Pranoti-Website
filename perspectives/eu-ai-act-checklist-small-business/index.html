EU AI Act Checklist for Small Business: 6 Articles That Matter

A practical EU AI Act checklist for small business — the six articles that actually apply, what each demands, and links to the full guide on each one.

31 Jul 2026 ·6 min read ·Pranoti Kshirsagar
EU AI ActSME complianceAI regulationsmall businesscompliance checklist

Most SMEs and agencies I talk to have mentally filed the EU AI Act as someone else’s problem — 180+ articles of dense legal text, clearly written for hyperscalers and regulators, not a five-person creative studio or a research consultancy. That’s the wrong read. This is a practical EU AI Act checklist for small business: six articles decide almost everything a small team needs to know, and this is the one-page map to them.

I’ve written a full deep-dive on each of these six separately. This piece is the index — read it first, then follow whichever link matches where you actually are.

Key takeaways: Two of these six obligations (Articles 4 and 5) have already applied since February 2025 — not a future deadline. One article (62) exists purely to help you, not burden you. And if your agency rebrands, fine-tunes, or repurposes any AI tool for a client, Article 25 is the one clause most teams have never read but most need to.

Art. 3 — Are you even in scope?

Before anything else, Article 3 sets out the roles the entire Act is built on: a provider develops an AI system and places it on the market; a deployer uses one under its own authority. Most of the Act’s obligations hinge entirely on which side of that line you sit on.

For a deeper walkthrough of how to classify your own tools, see the official 7-element test for what counts as an “AI system” and the provider-vs-deployer test for the GPAI Code of Practice.

Art. 4 — You already have a literacy duty

Article 4 requires providers and deployers to ensure “a sufficient level of AI literacy” among staff and anyone operating AI systems on their behalf — including freelancers and contractors. This has applied since 2 February 2025, six months earlier than most teams assume.

Full breakdown, including what “sufficient” actually means in practice: Article 4 for creator agencies and small teams.

Art. 5 — The red lines that apply to everyone, today

Article 5 has also applied since 2 February 2025, and bans specific practices outright — no risk assessment, no exception for small operators. The four that realistically touch an ordinary tool stack: workplace emotion recognition, manipulative dark-pattern design, untargeted facial-image scraping, and biometric categorisation by protected traits. Two more bans — on AI-generated non-consensual intimate images and CSAM — were added by the July 2026 Omnibus amendment and apply from 2 December 2026.

Full checklist to run your own tools through: the 4 Article 5 practices you’re banned from using.

Art. 25 — How your agency could accidentally become a “provider”

Article 25 is the one clause most agencies never read. Rebrand a tool under your own name, substantially modify it, or repurpose a general-purpose model into a high-risk use, and you can legally become the “provider” — inheriting the full conformity-assessment and documentation burden that was supposed to sit with your vendor.

Full breakdown of the three triggers and the contract clause that decides who carries the risk: Article 25 — when you become the AI “provider”.

Art. 50 — The one deadline everyone’s heard of

Article 50 requires clear, distinguishable disclosure when content is AI-generated or manipulated, before a reader’s first exposure to it — not buried in end credits or a linked policy page. This is the transparency obligation most creator-facing coverage of the Act focuses on, and it applies from 2 August 2026.

Full placement guidance for blog posts, social media, video, and podcasts: how to add AI disclosures that comply with the EU AI Act.

Art. 62 — The support most teams don’t know they can claim

Article 62 is the part of the Act that runs in the small team’s favour: priority access to national regulatory sandboxes, reduced conformity-assessment fees, a direct advice channel with your national authority, and free standardised templates from the AI Office.

Full breakdown of how to actually claim each measure: Article 62 — free support SMEs can claim.

Common questions on the EU AI Act for small business

Does the EU AI Act actually apply to a business with under 10 people? Yes. Size doesn’t exempt you from the Act’s obligations — it changes how those obligations are enforced. Article 99(6) caps fines for SMEs and start-ups at the lower of the applicable percentage or amount thresholds, rather than the higher figures large companies face, and Article 62 gives you support most teams never claim.

What’s the single most-missed obligation for small teams? Article 25. Most agencies assume they’re a “deployer” using someone else’s tool, and never realise that rebranding, substantially modifying, or repurposing a tool can legally make them the “provider” instead — with the full compliance burden that entails.

Do I need to worry about fines right now? For most small teams, no — the highest-profile deadlines (Article 50 transparency, most Annex III high-risk obligations) either haven’t arrived yet or have been pushed to December 2027 under the 2026 Omnibus amendments. Articles 4 and 5 are the exception: both have applied since February 2025.

Where do I start if I’ve done nothing about this yet? Run the 7-element test on your actual tool stack first — it tells you whether the Act applies to a given tool at all, before you worry about any specific article.

How to use this map

Bookmark this page rather than any single article — the Act’s obligations phase in over several years, and which of these six matters most to you will shift as your team, tools, and deadlines change. Revisit it quarterly, or whenever you adopt a new AI tool or take on a new type of client work.

About the host

I'm a materials scientist by training — PhD from Tübingen, work at the interface of materials science and life sciences — and when I started my undergrad in metallurgical and materials engineering, most people I told thought I meant meteorology. Physics has Cosmos. Biology has Attenborough. Materials and nanoscience — the field behind almost everything else — had almost no public voice. So in 2019, I built one.

The entry requirement for a guest isn't a materials-science degree — it's that their research happens at the micron scale or below, which has brought biochemists, physicists, and biologists onto the show alongside materials scientists. And the format is deliberately human-first: I ask about the person behind the research, not just the research, because that's what actually makes science land with people.

I run the whole thing myself — concept, questions, editorial line, even on sponsored episodes — self-funded since day one. 226 episodes and 132 guests in, listener feedback changed the format once already: in 2022 I added video, because people wanted to see the scientists they were hearing from.
